Synthesis
---------

  Synthesis is the process of breaking down your VHDL (or Verilog) code and converting it to simple components (LUTs, flip-flops, block RAMs and so on) that exists on your specific FPGA.
In this sense, an FPGA synthesis tool is similar to a compiler, which takes code ina language like C and breaks it down into very simple instructions that your cpu can understand.

A key output of synthesis process is your utilization report, which tells you how many {FPGA resources: LUTs, flip-flops, block RAMs and other resources} you're using in your design.

errors after synthesis:
(1) Syntax errors
(2) Utilization error:
    where your design requires more components than the FPGA has available.
        (a) Switch to a larger FPGA
        (b) Identify the most resource-intensive modules and rewrite them.
        (c) Remove functionality.
b:(i) rewriting the code with a focus on lower reesource utilization.
  (ii) have different inputs (or channels) share the same FPGA resource. [only works if you have time available to share a resource.]

The process of sharing a resource is called #arbitration, and the component that performs sharing is often referred to as an #arbiter.
Arbiters can be built to share off-FPGA resources as well.
