FPGA Clock Domain Crossing (CDC)
================================

Clock Domain Crossing (CDC) in FPGA design refers to transferring signals or data between two clock domains operating at different frequencies or phases. 
This process is critical in FPGA designs to avoid metastability and timing errors, which can lead to unpredictable behavior.

Challenges in CDC
-----------------
When signals cross clock domains, setup and hold time violations can occur, leading to metastability. 
Metastability is a temporary unstable state in flip-flops, which can propagate errors through the design. 
Proper techniques are required to mitigate these issues.

Techniques for CDC
==================

Single Signal Transfer
---===================

Double-flop synchronization
---------------------------
For transferring individual signals between clock domains, the double-flop synchronization method is commonly used. 
This involves passing the signal through two flip-flops in the destination clock domain. 
The first flip-flop may experience metastability, but the second ensures a stable output.

Example in Verilog:
  always @(posedge i_Fast_Clock) begin
  r1_Data <= i_Slow_Data; // Metastable
  r2_Data <= r1_Data; // Stable
  end

Pulse Stretching
----------------
When transferring pulses from a faster clock domain to a slower one, 
the pulse must be stretched to ensure it is sampled correctly. 
The pulse duration should be extended to at least two cycles of the slower clock to meet setup and hold requirements.

FIFO for Bulk Data Transfer
---========================
For transferring large amounts of data, a FIFO (First-In-First-Out) buffer is the most reliable method. 
A dual-clock FIFO allows independent read and write operations in different clock domains. Key considerations include:

* Almost Full (AF) and Almost Empty (AE) Flags: These flags help manage data flow without relying on Full or Empty signals, which can be less reliable.
* Timing Constraints: Proper constraints must be applied to inform the FPGA tools about the CDC, ensuring metastability is handled correctly.

Timing Constraints
------------------
FPGA tools often flag timing errors during CDC. These errors indicate potential metastability. 
Designers must create timing exceptions (e.g., set_false_path) to relax constraints for CDC paths, signaling that the design accounts for metastability.

Best Practices
---===========
* Avoid mixing signals from different clock domains in the same logic.
* Use double-flopping for single-bit signals and FIFOs for multi-bit data.
* Stretch pulses when transferring from fast to slow clock domains.
* Apply proper timing constraints to avoid unnecessary tool warnings.

Understanding and implementing these techniques ensures reliable communication between clock domains in FPGA designs.
